Revised: 2026-08-21 — **non-flagship discretionary hours: row retired, one day after it was
added.** Original row, preserved: `| non-flagship discretionary hours | >4h/week | weekly
check-in estimate, DRI-corrected |`. It was added 2026-08-20, read once at that day's check-in,
and is retired here. The retro should see that as the fast reversal it is.

**Why it goes.** Three reasons, in order of weight.

1. **It measured the DRI, not okrdev.** The other three rows are properties of the framework —
   cross-file coherence, install footprint, coach nag rate. This one measured how a person spent
   his week. That is the mismatch underneath every other problem it had, and the principle worth
   keeping is the general one: *okrdev's health metrics monitor okrdev's health, not its DRI's.*
2. **It had no instrument.** With committed floors exempt, the non-exempt residual is roughly
   four hours out of thirty-odd hours of non-flagship time — so it reads at its own red line
   almost by construction. The check-in that read it produced two figures that disagreed about
   fourfold, for the same reason: commit counts over-weight many small commits and under-weight
   large work landing in few.
3. **It could not be explained without breaching the partition.** Reporting a red reading means
   saying where the hours went, and that is other businesses — in a public repo. That exposure
   is the same one [issue #24](https://github.com/backedbydata-co/okrdev/issues/24) was promoted
   to close, and a monitored metric should not require the thing another KR is fixing.

**Why this is not a red line argued down during its own breach.** The row was at its line when
retired, and [method.md](../../docs/method.md#amendments--the-mid-cycle-change-protocol) forbids
renegotiating a line while it is red. What makes this a retirement rather than a renegotiation
is that **the measurement is not being removed, it is being superseded by a stricter one that
already exists** — a capacity model in the DRI's private portfolio state that prices every
standing commitment against a real working week. It reads worse than this row did, not better:
this row reported a reading at its line, and the model it hands off to reports that committed
floors consume nearly the whole core week before any flagship work at all.

That distinction is the whole of it, so it is written as a condition rather than left as a
precedent: **a monitored metric may be retired mid-cycle only into a replacement that already
exists and is at least as demanding.** Retiring one into nothing, or into something laxer, is
the failure mode the red-line rule names, and this clause is what keeps the two apart.

**What is not affected.** No other red line moves. The three remaining rows are unchanged. The
2026-W34 check-in keeps its reading of this row verbatim: it was held while the row existed, and
a held check-in is a record, not a live document.

reading red — W32's ~7.5h is above the 4h line before the metric exists — which is the point
of installing it rather than an argument against it. The protocol's "never renegotiate a red
line during its own breach" rule is not engaged: that rule guards against a line being argued
*down* while red, and this line is being argued into existence.
